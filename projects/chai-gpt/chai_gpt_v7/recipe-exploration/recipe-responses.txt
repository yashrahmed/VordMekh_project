Recipe for Masala Chai

Ingredients:  
1. Water – 8 cups  
2. Whole milk – 4 cups (adjust for desired richness)  
3. Loose black tea (Assam or Darjeeling) – 6 tablespoons  
4. Fresh ginger – 2 tablespoons, grated or crushed  
5. Green cardamom pods – 8, lightly crushed  
6. Cinnamon sticks – 2 large (or 3 small)  
7. Cloves – 8  
8. Black peppercorns – 1 teaspoon, lightly crushed  
9. Fennel seeds – 2 teaspoons, lightly crushed  
10. Star anise – 2 whole pieces  
11. Sugar – 6 to 8 tablespoons (adjust to taste)  

Preparation steps:  
1. In a large but manageable pot, add 8 cups of water and bring to a gentle boil over medium heat.  
2. Add ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, black peppercorns, fennel seeds, and star anise. Simmer for 5–7 minutes until aromatic.  
3. Add the loose black tea to the pot. Simmer another 2–3 minutes, ensuring it doesn’t boil over.  
4. Slowly pour in the milk and return to a gentle simmer. Watch carefully to avoid spilling as the volume and foam increase.  
5. Add sugar and stir well until dissolved. Taste and adjust sweetness or spice strength if needed.  
6. Turn off the heat and let the chai rest for a minute or two so the flavors deepen.  
7. Strain carefully into a lighter vessel or large teapot using a fine sieve. Work in batches if lifting is difficult.  
8. Serve hot in cups or insulated carafes for easy pouring.  

Tip: If lifting the pot is heavy, use a ladle to transfer the chai gradually into strainers placed over serving containers.

Recipe for Kashmiri Chai

Ingredients:
1. Kashmiri tea leaves (noon chai leaves or gunpowder green tea) – 6 tablespoons  
2. Water – 10 cups  
3. Baking soda – 1 teaspoon  
4. Cold water (for shocking tea) – 5 cups  
5. Milk – 10 cups (preferably full-fat)  
6. Sugar – 1¼ cups (adjust to taste)  
7. Salt – ½ teaspoon (traditional flavor balance)  
8. Green cardamom pods – 12, lightly crushed  
9. Saffron threads – a generous pinch (about ½ teaspoon), soaked in 2 tablespoons warm milk  
10. Chopped pistachios – ¼ cup (for garnish)  
11. Chopped almonds – ¼ cup (for garnish; optional)  

Preparation steps:
1. In a large but manageable heavy-bottomed pot, bring 10 cups of water to a boil.  
2. Add the Kashmiri tea leaves and baking soda. Boil on medium-high heat for 10–12 minutes, stirring gently with a wooden spoon. The tea will deepen in color.  
3. Add 5 cups of cold water and whisk or stir briskly for 1–2 minutes to help release the pinkish tones. Continue simmering another 8–10 minutes until the liquid reduces slightly.  
4. Strain the tea base into another pot to remove tea leaves. This is your concentrated “Kahwa” or tea extract.  
5. In a separate large, lighter pot (or divide into two if easier to manage), bring milk to a gentle boil. Add the strained tea extract, sugar, salt, and crushed cardamom. Simmer on low for 5–10 minutes, stirring occasionally.  
6. Add the saffron-infused milk and simmer another 2–3 minutes for color and aroma.  
7. Taste and adjust sweetness or salt if needed.  
8. Remove from heat and serve hot in cups with chopped pistachios and almonds sprinkled on top.  

*Tip:* If handling large volumes is difficult, prepare the tea extract in advance, refrigerate, and mix with hot milk in smaller batches during serving time.

3. Whole milk – 2.5 liters (equal ratio to water; adjust creaminess as desired)  
4. Sugar – 10–12 teaspoons (or to taste; can be added later)  
5. Cinnamon sticks – 3 large or 4 medium  
6. Vanilla extract – 2 teaspoons (or 1 split vanilla bean if available)  
7. Optional: crushed cardamom pods – 6–8 for extra depth  
8. Optional: small pinch of salt to enhance flavor  

---

**Preparation steps:**  
1. **Prepare the brewing base:** In a large but manageable pot, bring 2.5 liters of water to a gentle boil over medium heat.  
2. **Add spices:** Add cinnamon sticks (and cardamom if using). Let them simmer for about 5–7 minutes until the aroma deepens.  
3. **Add tea leaves:** Stir in the Nilgiri tea leaves. Reduce heat and simmer for 3–4 minutes.  
4. **Incorporate milk:** Slowly pour in the milk. Stir well and maintain a gentle simmer — do not let it boil over.  
5. **Infuse flavor:** Add vanilla extract (or split vanilla bean). Let the mixture gently simmer for 5–7 minutes, stirring occasionally.  
6. **Sweeten:** Add sugar to taste, stirring until dissolved.  
7. **Strain:** Carefully pour the chai through a fine mesh strainer into a heatproof container or teapot. Work in batches if heavy pots are difficult to handle — use a ladle to transfer liquid for straining instead of lifting the entire pot.  
8. **Serve:** Serve hot. Keep covered to maintain warmth if serving in multiple rounds.  

*Tip: If preparing well in advance, store the brewed chai without milk and refrigerate. Add hot milk and reheat gently before serving to preserve freshness and reduce heavy pot handling.*

Recipe for Nilgiri Chai

Ingredients:
1. Nilgiri tea leaves – 2 teaspoons  
2. Water – 1½ cups  
3. Milk – 1 cup (whole or your preferred type)  
4. Sugar or sweetener – 1 to 2 teaspoons (adjust to taste)  
5. Cinnamon stick – 1 small piece (about 2–3 cm) or ¼ teaspoon ground cinnamon  
6. Vanilla extract – ¼ teaspoon (or a few drops of vanilla essence)

Preparation steps:
1. Place a small to medium saucepan on the stove; pour in the water and add the cinnamon stick (or ground cinnamon).  
2. Bring the water to a gentle boil over medium heat; allow the cinnamon to simmer for 2–3 minutes to release its flavor.  
3. Add the Nilgiri tea leaves to the simmering water. Let them steep for 2–3 minutes.  
4. Add the milk and desired amount of sugar or sweetener. Stir gently and bring the mixture just to a boil, watching carefully to avoid overflow.  
5. Lower the heat and simmer for another minute to allow the flavors to blend.  
6. Turn off the heat and add the vanilla extract. Stir gently.  
7. Strain the chai into cups using a small mesh strainer.  
8. Serve hot and enjoy your floral-spiced Nilgiri Chai.

Recipe for Nilgiri Chai

**Ingredients:**  
1. Nilgiri tea leaves – 10 tablespoons (about 1 tablespoon per 2 cups water)  
2. Water – 20 cups (for about 10 large servings)  
3. Milk (optional, for creamy version) – up to 10 cups (replace half the water if using)  
4. Sugar – ½ to ¾ cup (adjust to taste)  
5. Green cardamom pods – 8 to 10 (lightly crushed, optional)  
6. Fresh ginger – 2-inch piece (sliced or crushed, optional)  

**Preparation steps:**  
1. Choose a large pot that you can comfortably lift even when half full. To make handling easier, consider boiling in two batches if your pot is small.  
2. Pour the water (and milk if making the creamy version) into the pot. Heat on medium flame.  
3. If adding ginger and cardamom, place them in the liquid as it warms.  
4. When the mixture starts to boil gently, add Nilgiri tea leaves.  
5. Reduce heat slightly and let it simmer for 3–4 minutes for a lighter brew, or up to 5 minutes if you prefer stronger tea.  
6. Add sugar and stir until it dissolves fully. Taste and adjust sweetness.  
7. Turn off heat.  
8. Strain the tea carefully into a large, heatproof container or directly into serving flasks using a fine strainer or mesh sieve (use a lightweight ladle if lifting is difficult).  
9. Serve hot. Keep the tea covered to retain warmth. Add extra hot water or milk later if needed to stretch more servings while maintaining balance.

Recipe for Nilgiri Chai

**Ingredients:**  
1. Nilgiri tea leaves – 10 tablespoons (about 1 tablespoon per person)  
2. Water – 2.5 liters  
3. Whole milk – 1.5 liters (adjust based on preference for stronger or lighter chai)  
4. Sugar – 6 to 8 tablespoons (for mild sweetness; adjust slightly to taste)  
5. Fresh ginger (optional) – 2-inch piece, lightly crushed  
6. Green cardamom pods (optional) – 6, slightly crushed  

**Preparation steps:**  
1. In a large but manageable pot (around 4–5 liters capacity), pour in the water and bring it to a gentle boil.  
2. Add crushed ginger and cardamom pods if using; simmer for 2–3 minutes to release their aroma.  
3. Add the Nilgiri tea leaves to the pot and simmer on medium-low heat for 2–3 minutes until the tea releases its color and aroma.  
4. Pour in the milk carefully, stirring slowly; bring the mixture to a gentle simmer again.  
5. Stir in the sugar and let the chai simmer for another 2–3 minutes, ensuring it doesn’t boil over.  
6. Turn off the heat and allow the chai to rest for 1–2 minutes so the flavors balance.  
7. Using a medium mesh strainer, strain the tea directly into a heatproof jug or thermos, working in portions if needed to avoid handling heavy pots.  
8. Serve immediately or keep warm in thermos flasks for convenient serving to the group.

Recipe for Nilgiri Chai

Ingredients:  
1. Nilgiri tea leaves – 2 teaspoons (for 2 cups; adjust to taste)  
2. Water – 1½ cups  
3. Milk – 1 cup (use lighter milk if preferred)  
4. Sugar – 1 to 1½ teaspoons (mild sweetness; adjust to taste)  

Preparation steps:  
1. Pour water into a small, lightweight saucepan and bring it to a gentle boil over medium heat.  
2. Add the Nilgiri tea leaves. Let them simmer for about 2 minutes until the aroma rises.  
3. Add milk to the pan and allow the mixture to come to a gentle boil again.  
4. Lower the heat and simmer for another 2–3 minutes to let the tea develop its brisk flavor.  
5. Add sugar and stir until fully dissolved. Taste and adjust sweetness if needed.  
6. Turn off the heat. Using a small strainer, carefully pour the tea into cups.  
7. Serve hot, savoring the balanced briskness and mild sweetness.
